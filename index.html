From Inscription and Ink to Print and Code

A Comparative Analysis of Tanakh Textual Variants and Embedded Masorah

Masorah maps three millennia of text transmission (2nd Millennium BCE to 1525 CE) across seven distinct evidentiary tiers. By synthesizing digital, material, and statistical methodologies with classical philology, the platform examines the Masoretic Text and Dead Sea Scrolls traditions within a triangulated provenance framework. From early Proto-Semitic inscriptions to the 1525 Rabbinic Bible baseline, eCBGM analysis and the decoding of the "Masoretic Code" bring unprecedented computational clarity to Tanakh textual variants.

The Project

Seven Tiers of Textual Witness

The corpus organizes manuscript, print, and translation evidence into a seven-tier evidentiary framework, evaluated through structured comparison of extant witnesses rather than a single reconstructed archetype.

TIER 01

Proto-Semitic Roots

Umm el-Marra, Wadi el-Hol, Serabit el-Khadim, Proto-Canaanite, Mesha Stele, Ketef Hinnom

TIER 02

Second Temple Witnesses

Dead Sea Scrolls, Great Isaiah Scroll, Samaritan Pentateuch, Cairo Genizah fragments, and related evidence

TIER 03

Masoretic Traditions

Aleppo Codex, Leningrad Codex, Sassoon 1053, MS Cairensis, and the Tiberian/Babylonian/Palestinian sub-traditions

TIER 04

Sephardic Targum

Sephardic targumic witnesses bridging the Masoretic and printed traditions

TIER 05

Printed & Critical

Rabbinic Bibles (1517-1548), BHS, BHQ, and the 1525 Second Rabbinic Bible baseline

TIER 06

Translations

Septuagint, Peshitta, Vulgate, and major English versions (KJV, JPS, ESV)

TIER 07

Commentaries

Historical and modern critical commentary informing variant interpretation
